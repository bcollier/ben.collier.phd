01 — About

The model was never the decision.

Interests

Experimentation and causal inference. The sociology of open collaboration, which was my dissertation territory and still shows up in my published work on Wikipedia. How technical teams get assembled and assessed. Whether any dashboard has ever changed a decision.

Tools

Python with pandas and scikit-learn, daily. SQL and the data modelling underneath it. Large language models applied to text, image, and network data, taught as instruments with known failure modes rather than as oracles.

Education

PhD and MS, Information Systems and Organizational Behavior, Carnegie Mellon. MBA, University of Wisconsin–Madison. BBA in Management Computer Systems and Mathematics, Wisconsin–Whitewater.

My appointment is at the Tepper School of Business, where I teach across the MBA, the MS in Business Analytics, and undergraduate programs, plus selected courses at Heinz College. The PhD is from Carnegie Mellon in Information Systems and Organizational Behavior, which is a long way of saying I arrived at machine learning through the question of how organizations actually behave rather than the other way round.

That order is the whole method. My labs spend as much time on whether a finding should change anyone's behaviour as on whether the model converged, because a classifier with a good score that nobody can act on is a hobby.

Before returning to the faculty I led data science at Duolingo through the IPO and the launch of Duolingo Max, running monetization analytics, large-scale experimentation, and financial forecasting. Before that I ran data science at UPMC as founding data scientist on a joint venture with IBM Watson Health, leading CognitiveRx, a drug price forecasting and shortage prediction product later acquired by Premier. Earlier still, four years at Carnegie Mellon Qatar teaching organizational behavior and co-directing executive education.

The practice work continues through Hot Metal AI, my consulting and corporate training firm, and gAIm Systems, where I build tools for how sports organizations recruit, develop, and assemble teams. It is what keeps the labs from drifting into toy problems.

In 2026 the graduating MBA class voted me the George Leland Bach Teaching Award. That one belongs to them more than to me.
