Professor argues the model was never the decision

Ben Collier teaches business analytics at the Tepper School and Heinz College, and keeps a consulting practice running so the labs never drift into toy problems.

By Ben Collier  ·  Assistant Teaching Professor of Business Analytics  ·  Tepper School of Business

Continued in three columns below. Course listings on this page. Consulting rates in the classified section. Correspondence welcome.

My appointment is at the Tepper School of Business, where I teach across the MBA, the MS in Business Analytics, and undergraduate programs, with selected courses at Heinz College. The PhD is from Carnegie Mellon in Information Systems and Organizational Behavior, which is a long way of saying I arrived at machine learning through the question of how organizations actually behave rather than the other way round.

That order matters. It is why my labs spend as much time on whether a finding should change anyone's behaviour as on whether the model converged. A classifier with a good AUC that nobody can act on is a hobby.

Before returning to the faculty I led data science at Duolingo through the IPO and the launch of Duolingo Max, running monetization analytics, large-scale experimentation, and financial forecasting. Before that I was at UPMC as Senior Director of Data Science and the founding data scientist on a joint venture with IBM Watson Health, where I led CognitiveRx, a drug price forecasting and shortage prediction product later acquired by Premier.

A classifier with a good AUC that nobody can act on is a hobby.

Earlier still I spent four years at Carnegie Mellon Qatar as Assistant Teaching Professor of Organizational Behavior and Co-Director of Executive Education, teaching negotiation, leadership, and research methods to students and executives across the Gulf.

The practice work continues today through Hot Metal AI, my analytics consulting and corporate training firm, and through gAIm Systems, where I am Senior Director of AI and Data Science building tools for how sports organizations recruit, develop, and assemble teams.

In 2026 the graduating MBA class voted me the George Leland Bach Teaching Award. That one is theirs more than mine, and it is the piece of this page I am least neutral about.

Interests. Experimentation and causal inference; the sociology of open collaboration, which was my dissertation territory and still shows up in my published work on Wikipedia; how technical teams get assembled and assessed; and the stubborn question of whether any dashboard has ever changed a decision.

Tools. Python with pandas and scikit-learn daily. SQL and the unglamorous data modelling underneath it. Tableau when the audience needs it. Increasingly, large language models applied to text, image, and network data, taught as instruments with known failure modes rather than as oracles.
