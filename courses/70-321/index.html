Negotiation and Conflict Resolution

A negotiation every week, a debrief every week, and a real negotiation of your own at the end.

About 20 students

Every Monday students negotiated a role-play case against a classmate, and every Wednesday we took apart what happened: who claimed value, who created it, and what they left on the table. The cases built from a two-party price haggle to multi-party coalitions, mediation, social dilemmas, and a cross-cultural deal over a theme park. Alongside the cases, each student started the semester with a single KitKat and traded up as far as they could. The course ended with a paper on a real negotiation each student planned and carried out outside class.

No. 03

What students hand in

Major assignments

Real world negotiation paper 35%

Plan and carry out a negotiation outside class for anything you would actually be willing to buy or sell, then write it up in 2,000 words or fewer. The best papers built a scoring system for their own interests, estimated the other side's reservation price with honest uncertainty, and were self-critical about what they left on the table.

Post-negotiation journals 25%

Three two-page journals over the semester, each sent to the instructor and to the other people in that negotiation. The point was reflection on what you were thinking, not a blow-by-blow account, and the counterpart's journal gave a rare view from the other side.

Pop quizzes 15%

Five quizzes through the semester on the readings and concepts.

Attendance and class discussion 15%

Every negotiation needs a prepared partner, so missing one without arranging a substitute cost a third of a letter grade.

Negotiation plans 10%

A planning document handed in for each in-class negotiation: issues and their values, BATNA, reservation price, target, sources of power, and an opening move.

KitKat trade-up

Modeled on the One Red Paperclip story. Each student started with a KitKat and made at least three trades, logging every one, then wrote a one-page analysis of the tactics they used. On the last day the class voted on the best final item. It counted toward participation.
