Major assignments

  • Problem sets40%

    Six weekly sets of business problems, solved in Excel or Python and submitted on Canvas. Students could work through them with their assigned MSBA group but submitted individually. The problems include who wins a seven-game World Series, how long it takes to roll doubles to get out of jail in Monopoly, how many ambulances a service needs when calls arrive every 75 minutes on average, value at risk for a portfolio, lognormal insurance claims, and whether coffee and soda prices move together.

  • Final exam60%

    An open-book, open-notes exam taken on Canvas in the final exam period, with questions modeled on the problem sets, quizzes, and class examples.

  • In-class quizzesExtra credit, up to 20%

    Fifteen to twenty one-point quizzes taken on laptops during class, using the ideas covered that day.

  • Section quizzesExtra credit, up to 9%

    Three optional Canvas quizzes, each covering the previous two weeks of material.

  • Practice sets, summaries, and walkthrough videos

    Added halfway through the term: practice problem sets with solutions for interval estimation, hypothesis testing, and regression, a written summary of each module and a combined formula sheet, and recorded walkthroughs of the harder problems, such as the airline overbooking expected value problem.

How the course works

Every distribution is taught twice, once with its Excel formula and once with its Python call, and nearly every example is a business decision: overbooking a flight, a cosmetics launch whose chance of success moves from 30% to 60% after a market test, tea bottles that must hold 750 ml. Sessions open with a puzzle or a question from quant interviews.

Halfway through the seven-week term I changed the pace: more worked examples in class, practice sets with solutions, a short written summary of each module, and walkthrough videos for the hardest problems.

How the course developed

I taught the MS in Business Analytics program's first-term statistics course in Fall 2024, in two morning sections. I reworked the slide decks I inherited and used Excel and Python alongside the textbook, so that every idea had both a formula and a working example.
