70-453 Business Technology Consulting

70-453

How consultants think, how they interview, and how technology projects get built and managed.

15 students in 2014 and 7 in 2015

I built this course for CMU Qatar from an older systems analysis and design course, and turned it toward both sides of consulting: the business analysis that consulting firms hire for, and the technology work of designing and running a project. The first half was frameworks and case interviews, with Harvard cases on Deloitte and BCG and live practice cases like Harley-Davidson and a fighter jet division. The second half ran a Harvard change management simulation and then moved into the systems development life cycle, UML, agile, and project management. Students in Business Administration, Information Systems, and Computer Science took it together, and it was built to get them ready for a first-round interview at firms like McKinsey, BCG, Accenture, and Bain.

Major assignments

  • Case interviews

    In 2015, each student prepared two interview scripts, ran four case interviews with a partner (two as interviewer, two as candidate), and wrote a three to five page analysis of strengths and improvements for each. A second round followed with a new partner, then a case interview and a behavioral interview with me.

  • Client project

    Teams in 2015 worked through a full engagement for a client inside the university: a client interview graded against a rubric, a five to eight page proposal with a work breakdown structure and cost estimate at set daily rates, a technical design in UML, a user interface design, and a working prototype.

  • Final consulting project

    In 2014, small groups chose a real organization and applied the course frameworks (profit, business situation, SWOT, Porter) to a problem it faced, presented during the final exam period.

  • Simulations

    Harvard's change management simulation, run once in a low-urgency and once in a high-urgency scenario, plus project management and strategy simulations in 2015.

  • Exams

    Closed-book exams, one-third short answer and two-thirds consulting case analysis. In 2014 the midterm and final were 30% and 40%; in 2015 a single final was 20%.

Student work

Every project, by term.

Spring 2014: final consulting projects 5 projects
  • Vodafone Qatar. Looked at Vodafone's launch of Falla, the first Qatari telecom plan aimed at young people, and asked which youth products and services Vodafone should focus on to position itself against Ooredoo. Porter's five forces and a youth-market strategy
  • Zara. Analyzed Zara's vertically integrated, fast-turnover model, including its single distribution center and weak online presence, and recommended where it should grow next. Business situation framework and SWOT
  • A GCC charity supporting Syrian refugees. The charity raised most of its money through events, an unstable source. The team estimated its revenues and costs and recommended ways to increase donations and build a steadier income. Profit framework, SWOT, and business situation framework
  • A yoga studio in Doha. Asked how the studio could grow with the market, cross-sell, and attract new customers, and recommended partnerships with sports and hotel facilities, corporate yoga, and better merchandising. SWOT, competitive comparison, BCG matrix, and Porter's five forces
  • A long-established Doha electronics and video game retailer. Compared revenue and costs from 2011 to 2012, and weighed small stores, limited capital, and the shift to digital games against the store's reputation and locations. Profit analysis from interviews with the general manager and an accountant
Spring 2015: client technology projects 2 projects
  • Visualizing Twitter for academic assessment. A tool to pull a Twitter stream by search term, sort it into threads, and let an instructor grade students' contributions to discussion outside class. Client interview, proposal, UML design, UI design, prototype
  • Digitizing negotiation exercises. A web platform, aimed at the iPad, for running classroom negotiation exercises like New Recruit so that results and scoring are immediate instead of done on paper. Client interview, proposal, UML design, UI design, prototype
