Managing and Assessing Tech Talent and Organizations

How to hire, develop, and evaluate technical people using evidence instead of instinct.

A micro-course I developed on managing technical talent: how to interview for technical and data roles, onboard new hires, run technical projects with OKRs, and manage performance. I wrote it for undergraduates, and it ran for the first time at CMU Qatar in Fall 2025.

No. 03

Method

How the course works

Students sit on both sides of the interview table. In live mock interviews on Zoom they rotate through interviewer, candidate, and observer, using the formats large tech companies use: live coding, data take-homes, case interviews, and behavioral interviews. Candidates may solve the coding problems in a spreadsheet, so business students take part alongside computer science students.

The in-person week in Doha turned to teams and culture. Groups allocated a $120 million fund across twenty proposals, analyzed a Harvard case on a global team at Sun Microsystems, and rescued a failing mobile banking project by cutting its scope and writing recovery OKRs.

No. 04

What students hand in

Major assignments

Mock interviews and reflections 50%

Students rotate through interviewer, candidate, and observer in three rounds: a coding interview, a data task built on the Cookie Cats mobile game A/B test, and a consulting-style case (Heartcorp, pricing a new cardiac stent). The interviewer scores the candidate against a rubric, and everyone writes a one to two page reflection on what the other side of the table taught them.

Onboarding plan

A two to three page 30/60/90-day onboarding guide for a new technical hire, built around a small first win, or a critique and redesign of a real company's public onboarding program such as GitLab's.

Group case analyses 40%

Cases worked in teams during the in-person week in Doha. In the Doha Foundation case, a funding board has $120 million and twenty reviewed proposals; each group uses an assigned decision method, from the rational model to dialectical inquiry to Delphi, and reports what it learned about deciding across cultures. In Managing a Global Team, a 45-person support team at Sun Microsystems spread across four countries misses an outage; teams diagnose the root causes and present a 30-day plan. Project Phoenix is a mobile banking app at month four of six, with 70% of the budget spent and 40% of the features done.

Management reflection 10%

A short paper on the strategy and tactics each student will use when they manage technical people.

No. 05

What students built

Student work

Every project, by term.

Group case: Managing a Global Team at Sun Microsystems, Fall 2025 3 projects
  • Team 1. One team traced the outage to unclear cross-site ownership, cultural misreadings and leadership communication. It recommended a single global escalation protocol, rebuilding the support queue with cross-site validation, a leads-only international meeting, site-by-site trust conversations and letting sites act without waiting for the U.S., with a global team charter of shared norms as the long-term change. Root causes, a 30-day plan, and one long-term change
  • Team 2. A second team blamed the absence of global process ownership, over-reliance on the U.S. team and cultural friction. It proposed manager-level cross-site reviews, sharing decisions with all sites at once, one emergency rule, a single owner for customer data and a rotating time-zone meeting schedule (U.S.-, Asia- and Euro-Asia-priority slots), plus an annual rotating in-person retreat and a unified Global Operations Playbook. Root causes, a 30-day plan, and one long-term change
  • Team 3. A third team stressed low trust, perceived inequity in task allocation and pay, and weak cultural awareness. It recommended a weekly global sync at rotating times with local pre-meetings, giving high-visibility work to the India and UAE sites, documenting decisions within 24 hours, a clear outage process and regional listening sessions with 1:1s, plus a company-wide 'Global Operating System' as the long-term change. Root causes, a 30-day plan, and one long-term change
No. 06

Revision history

How the course developed

I proposed this micro-course to Carnegie Mellon in Qatar in February 2025 and taught it in Fall 2025. Most sessions met on Zoom in the evening, Doha time, and the middle block met in person in Doha over three days. It was open to technical and business students with no prerequisites.
